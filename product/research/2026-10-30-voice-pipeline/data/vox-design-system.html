Type, three ways

All three are on Google Fonts. Instrument Sans is a calm grotesque with slightly narrow, crisp shapes. Instrument Serif comes from the same studio and is drawn to sit next to it, so a serif for the big moments doesn't clash with the sans.

A · Instrument Sans + Serifmy pick

The sans everywhere; the serif only for display and headline, which means the greeting, the app name and screen titles. That's where the warmth comes from, and it stays quiet everywhere else.

The mark, three ways

Each is a letterform from Instrument Serif Italic, converted to outlines so the icon doesn't depend on the font. Every option is shown at 29 and 60pt on a light home screen and a dark one.

1 · v, and a breathmy pick

An off-white italic v on the page colour, with one small teal dot like a held note or a full stop. The only colour is the dot, so it's a quiet icon on a busy home screen. The dark tile has a soft edge against black, so the glyph itself has to carry it.

2 · v on teal

The same v, dark on the accent. It's the easiest to find on any home screen, and louder than option 1.

3 · vox, the word

The whole name as a wordmark. Charming at 120pt, but at 29pt the o and x crowd together, so this one is better as the sign-in wordmark than as the icon.

The call

Vox is mostly a voice call with the agent, so the call screen is the heart of the app. The dot from the mark becomes the agent's presence: it's what you look at while you talk. Teal stays a state colour, meaning the agent is here and alive. There's no avatar and no face, just a voice made visible. The text chat stays as the quiet fallback.

Ouvindo

A hollow ring. A faint halo follows your voice, so you can see you're being heard.

Pensando

The dot breathes slowly. Nothing else moves.

Falando

Filled, with teal rings that swell with the agent's voice.
